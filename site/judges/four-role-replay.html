Four-role workflow (optional): do it yourself with four accounts

Starting point. This walkthrough uses a specific starting baseline. If earlier changes have been applied in this workspace, the sample values and expected results may no longer match. Check the current source revision and values before starting. For subsequent runs, follow the same workflow using the latest data.

You switch between four accounts that you receive separately (never written in this guide): a Requester, an Approver, a Reviewer and an Admin account. They all work in the same workspace as the one-role demo, on the same system. Applying a change here edits the stored source file, so the starting value, the history and the results change for everyone who comes after you. That is accepted and expected: read the current value first (Part II).

Your recommended path is still the one-role demo. The recorded case is the read-only evidence of an earlier run of this same workflow (a different rule and baseline).

Shared AI limits - read this first

All AI steps use one shared budget. An extra allowance for this path was added on 2026-10-16, aimed at about one to two four-role runs. The number of runs that complete is not guaranteed. Clarifications, a revised draft, extra questions and a wide re-read use it up faster. When a limit is reached Ripple shows the reason and charges nothing. Do not switch accounts or try to reset anything to get around it; if you cannot continue, read the stored results or the recorded case (reference material, not a completed run of yours). Details are in Part IV.

Part II - Continued exploration (any later run, any starting value)

  1. Check the baseline first, even on your first visit: someone may already have applied a change. Explorer, search approval.yaml, open the rule, read Value. In the Technical view (top right) the header shows the current Source revision.
  2. Start a new request from Changes reached from another page. Do not open a request that is not yours: My work lists the items waiting for the account you are signed in with.
  3. Write the request with the current value and a new value you choose. Read the AI's preview before you confirm; the AI may ask a question or word things differently, and you must not confirm a preview that has a wrong number.
  4. Read the simulation of this run as it is. How many cases change depends on the current value; do not expect the numbers of any example.
  5. Follow the same accounts and steps as in Part I.
  6. Out of date: if the source changed after your draft or proposal (someone applied), the page says so and an old approval cannot be used. Ask for a new draft ("Generate GenAI draft" again, or "Revise this draft…") instead.
  7. Partly processed (PARTIAL): not a failure. It means some sources stay with people; they remain listed as unresolved or potentially affected and are not treated as resolved.
  8. Verification and acceptance: an apply is not "done" until stage 9 has re-read the changed file and a person has accepted the knowledge and the result.
  9. Several people at once: each request has its own chain. If one change is applied, the others become out of date and need a new draft. If the page says jobs are waiting up to the workspace limit, wait and press the button again.
  10. Do not restore an earlier version to match an example. Restore is a separate administration step with its own approval and is not part of this workflow.

Part III - Administrator checks (optional; not part of the business workflow)

The Admin account can also try the administration functions. They are shared: the same system, workspace and service serve every judge and every other account. - AI and processing switches (Support & Access, card "AI and processing switches"). Two switches: New AI starts and Job processing. They are service-wide, not per workspace or per account. - Switch OFF "New AI starts": every new AI action of every user is refused with "AI actions are switched off on this server (by the operator)". Nothing is started or charged; saved results stay readable. - Switch OFF "Job processing": queued jobs wait and are not lost; jobs already running finish. - The header banner shows "New AI starts: ON/OFF - Job processing: ON/OFF" for every signed-in user, and the switch history lists who changed it and why. - To turn it back on: Support & Access, same card, type a reason of at least 10 characters, click Switch ON for each switch that is OFF. The banner returns to ON (it refreshes about every 30 seconds) and waiting jobs are picked up within seconds; a job left waiting is recovered by the periodic wake-up within a few minutes. - Switching off blocks every other judge until you switch it on again. At the end of the evaluation window the system closes the switches itself. - Members (Support & Access): reading the member list, and per-member AI use. A change affects that account for everyone using it. - Allocation and budget are not something a screen can raise; do not treat a refusal as a step to work around.

Part IV - The limits in detail

  • Per account and per conversation: at most 12 AI jobs per account per day and 6 AI turns per conversation.
  • Spending cap: As of 16 October 2026, the cumulative AI-provider spending cap for the shared demo project is USD 8.30. This includes prior usage; it is not a per-account allowance or a remaining balance.
  • Allowance added on 2026-10-16 for this path: up to 2 new extraction jobs and up to 3 new gap.answer jobs. An extraction job re-reads one source file after an apply; it is not a count of workflow runs. One apply can need several (up to all 13 files) and can use the whole allowance in a single run. A gap.answer job answers one "Ask on the new revision" (or one "Ask the AI to suggest") question. The aim was about one to two four-role runs; that is a serving target, not a guaranteed number of completed runs.
  • This is the ceiling of that grant, not what is left now. The allowance is shared by everyone who uses the workspace, and this static guide does not show the live balance. Other steps (chat, draft, simulation reading) have their own, larger allowances, and the project cap above covers everything together.
  • When one is used up the system shows the reason (for example "The approved allocation for this kind of AI action is used up") and charges nothing. Do not switch accounts or reset anything to get around it. Saved results stay readable; the recorded case is the reference if you cannot continue (it is not a completed run of yours).
  • After an apply: the source file is already changed (an apply is not undone). If the machine check of the changed file passed but the re-read did not finish, the state is "Applied and machine-verified; knowledge refresh incomplete": the knowledge is not fully updated and the result still needs a person's decision. Do not call it rolled back, fully verified or fully updated.
  • While something is pending: a job shows "Waiting to run" or "Running" and the page updates by itself.
  • Also shared: the number of jobs allowed to run at once in the workspace, the administrator switches (Part III) and the serving deadline 2026-11-06 23:59:59 SGT (= 15:59:59 UTC).
